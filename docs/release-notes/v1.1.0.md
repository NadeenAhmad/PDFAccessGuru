A bigger update: better structure for screen readers and better picture descriptions.

- **Nothing left out in partly tagged files.** Many PDFs come with tags for only part of their content (for example from a publisher's template). PDFAccessGuru now tags everything that was left out: text becomes paragraphs, headings or list items in the right place of the reading order, pictures become figures, and repeated headers, footers and page numbers are marked as decoration. In the test papers, the share of files that pass the PDF/UA check rose from a quarter to three quarters.
- **Charts and diagrams are found.** Charts and diagrams drawn with lines (common in papers) were hidden as decoration; now each one becomes one figure with a description, together with its labels. Many small pieces of one picture become one figure, and tiny decorations stay hidden. A paper that had 186 separate figures now has 5.
- **Formulas.** Equations set on their own line are tagged as formulas and get a text that says them in words (with an AI, from a picture of the formula).
- **Better headings.** Long paragraphs in bold or large type are no longer taken as headings, side notes such as "arXiv:…" are not headings, and heading levels follow the numbering without skipping a level.
- **Better lists.** Lists with bullets (also from symbol fonts), numbers, letters and reference lists ("[1] …") are found, also nested lists; the bullet or number is the item's label.
- **Captions.** Figure and table captions are tagged as captions, also when they run over several lines; a table's caption becomes part of the table, a figure's caption follows the figure.
- **You choose how detailed picture descriptions are.** In the app, box 2 now asks "How detailed should picture descriptions be?": **Brief** (one sentence for every picture), **Standard** (short for simple pictures, fuller for charts and diagrams; the default) or **Detailed** (more values and parts). In a terminal: `pdfaccessguru fix --descriptions brief|standard|detailed`. This applies when an AI writes the descriptions.
- **Clearer picture descriptions.** The AI is now asked for the meaning of a picture, not its looks: a colour is mentioned only when it stands for something, and then the description says what it stands for. A chart gets its main message first, then what is shown and the key values. Abbreviations that the document defines, for example "epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT)", are spelled out; abbreviations the document does not define are never guessed. Notation such as `q_alpha` or `x^2` is written so that screen readers can say it. A description that is too long is shortened after its last whole sentence.
- **Safer letters.** When a font does not say which letter a symbol is, PDFAccessGuru first uses the letter that PDF readers already show. An AI's guess is used only when it is a normal letter, digit, punctuation mark or symbol, never a control character or a rare technical sign.

- **No more waiting for a stuck online AI.** An AI answer that has not arrived after 4 minutes (15 minutes for an AI on your computer) now counts as failed, and simple rules write that text instead. Before, one stuck request could stop a file for hours.

To update: in the app, click **Update now** when it says that a new version is available. In a terminal: `pdfaccessguru update`.
